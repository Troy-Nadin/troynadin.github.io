About

I am a PhD student in Economics at Toronto Metropolitan University, working under the supervision of Dr. Brennan Thompson. My research studies the macro-finance of resource-dependent emerging economies: how sovereign default and restructuring, commodity price shocks, and financial-sector development shape credit, asset prices, and growth.

Before my doctoral studies, I spent four years in assurance and accounting, including at EY, working on audit, financial reporting under IFRS, and credit and covenant analysis. That experience shapes my empirical work: I care about measurement, institutional detail, and data that can be traced back to its source. I hold a Master of Finance from Saint Mary's University and am a CFA Level II candidate.

Research

Dissertation: Essays on the Macro-Finance of a Post-Default Resource-Dependent Economy: Empirical Evidence from Zambia

Zambia became the first African sovereign to default in the pandemic era and one of the first test cases of the G20 Common Framework. Its copper dependence and fast-growing mobile money sector make it a natural laboratory for studying how fiscal, external, and financial shocks interact.
